# Beginner Italian Lesson Plan (A1)

A structured 8-week plan for a complete beginner learning Italian, written in English. Each week builds on the previous one and includes vocabulary, grammar, and speaking practice.

---

## Course Overview

- **Level:** CEFR A1 (absolute beginner)
- **Duration:** 8 weeks, 3 sessions per week (about 30–45 minutes each)
- **Goal:** By the end, hold a simple conversation about yourself, order food, ask for directions, and understand basic spoken Italian.
- **Method:** Short, frequent sessions with lots of repetition and speaking out loud.

### How to use each session
1. **Warm-up (5 min):** Review the previous lesson out loud.
2. **New material (15 min):** Learn vocabulary and one grammar point.
3. **Practice (15 min):** Speak, write, or role-play.
4. **Cool-down (5 min):** Say what you learned without looking at notes.

---

## Week 1 — Greetings and Introductions

**Vocabulary:** ciao, buongiorno, buonasera, arrivederci, per favore, grazie, prego, sì, no, come stai?

**Grammar:**
- Subject pronouns: io, tu, lui/lei, noi, voi, loro
- The verb **essere** (to be): sono, sei, è, siamo, siete, sono
- The verb **chiamarsi** (to be called): mi chiamo, ti chiami…

**Practice:**
- Introduce yourself: "Ciao, mi chiamo ___. Sono ___."
- Ask and answer: "Come ti chiami?" / "Come stai?"

**Culture note:** Italians often use *ciao* for both hello and goodbye, but only with friends and family. Use *buongiorno* in formal settings.

---

## Week 2 — Numbers, Dates, and the Alphabet

**Vocabulary:** uno, due, tre, quattro, cinque, sei, sette, otto, nove, dieci… venti, trenta… cento. Days of the week. Months.

**Grammar:**
- The verb **avere** (to have): ho, hai, ha, abbiamo, avete, hanno
- Asking age: "Quanti anni hai?" — "Ho ___ anni."

**Practice:**
- Count to 100 out loud.
- Say your birthday: "Il mio compleanno è il ___."
- Spell your name using the Italian alphabet.

**Culture note:** The Italian alphabet has 21 letters; foreign letters (j, k, w, x, y) appear only in loanwords.

---

## Week 3 — People, Family, and Articles

**Vocabulary:** la famiglia, la madre, il padre, il fratello, la sorella, il figlio, la figlia, il nonno, la nonna, l'amico/l'amica.

**Grammar:**
- Definite articles: il, lo, la, l', i, gli, le
- Indefinite articles: un, uno, una, un'
- Noun gender (masculine/feminine) and number (singular/plural)

**Practice:**
- Describe your family: "Ho un fratello e due sorelle."
- Match the correct article to new nouns.

**Culture note:** Family is central to Italian culture; "la mamma" and "il papà" are common even for adults.

---

## Week 4 — Everyday Objects and Regular Verbs

**Vocabulary:** la casa, la cucina, il libro, il telefono, la chiave, la borsa, l'acqua, il pane, il caffè.

**Grammar:**
- Present tense of **-are** verbs: parlare → parlo, parli, parla, parliamo, parlate, parlano
- Negation with **non**: "Non parlo italiano."

**Practice:**
- Describe what is in your bag.
- Make 5 negative sentences about yourself.

**Culture note:** Coffee culture — ordering "un caffè" gets you an espresso by default.

---

## Week 5 — Food and Ordering

**Vocabulary:** il ristorante, il menù, il conto, l'antipasto, il primo, il secondo, il dolce, il vino, la pizza, il gelato.

**Grammar:**
- Polite requests with **vorrei** (I would like)
- The verb **prendere** (to take/have): prendo, prendi, prende…

**Practice:**
- Role-play ordering at a restaurant.
- Read a simple menu and ask for the bill: "Il conto, per favore."

**Culture note:** A full Italian meal has multiple courses; it is normal to linger over coffee after eating.

---

## Week 6 — Directions and the City

**Vocabulary:** la strada, la piazza, la stazione, a destra, a sinistra, dritto, vicino, lontano, dove?

**Grammar:**
- The verb **andare** (to go): vado, vai, va, andiamo, andate, vanno
- Prepositions of place: a, in, su, sotto, accanto a

**Practice:**
- Ask for directions: "Scusi, dov'è la stazione?"
- Give simple directions using a map.

**Culture note:** Italian cities often have a central *piazza* that serves as a meeting point.

---

## Week 7 — Daily Routine and Time

**Vocabulary:** la mattina, il pomeriggio, la sera, la notte, svegliarsi, mangiare, lavorare, dormire. Telling time.

**Grammar:**
- Reflexive verbs: mi sveglio, ti svegli, si sveglia…
- Telling time: "Sono le tre." / "È l'una."

**Practice:**
- Describe your day from morning to night.
- Ask and answer: "A che ora…?"

**Culture note:** Many shops close in the early afternoon for *la pausa* (riposo).

---

## Week 8 — Review and Conversation

**Vocabulary:** Review all previous sets.

**Grammar:**
- Passato prossimo (intro): "Ho mangiato", "Sono andato/a"
- Connecting ideas: e, ma, perché, quindi

**Practice:**
- Hold a 3–5 minute conversation combining all topics.
- Write a short paragraph about yourself in Italian.

**Culture note:** Regional differences are strong; greetings and food names change from north to south.

---

|-----|-------|

## Suggested Free Resources
- **Duolingo** or **Babbel** for daily drills
- **YouTube:** "Learn Italian with Lucrezia", "Italy Made Easy"
- **Podcasts:** "Coffee Break Italian"
- **Music:** Listen to Italian songs and read the lyrics

## Progress Checklist
- [ ] I can introduce myself and ask basic questions.
- [ ] I can count, tell time, and say dates.
- [ ] I can talk about my family and daily routine.
- [ ] I can order food and ask for the bill.
- [ ] I can ask for and understand simple directions.
- [ ] I can hold a short conversation in the present tense.

## Assessment Ideas
- **Week 4:** 10-question vocabulary quiz.
- **Week 6:** Record yourself giving directions.
- **Week 8:** 5-minute spoken conversation with a partner or tutor.
